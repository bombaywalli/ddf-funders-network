About this data

Each dot represents a funder that has backed a program with the Doris Duke Foundation (DDF). Each square represents one of those programs. A line means the funder is a member of that program. It shows shared membership, not a direct relationship between funders, and not how much the funder gave, which is mostly undisclosed.

Square size is the program's amount. Amounts mean different things by program (a pledge, a goal, a first round, a cumulative total); each program's card says which. Dashed squares and dashed lines mean no amount was found. Dot size is the funder's endowment (assets under management for FY23, or total assets from tax filings, 2023–2024). Hollow dots mean no figure was found, not that the funder is small. Dot color is the DDF program area, split when a funder backs programs in more than one area.

Each program card says whether it is a pooled fund (money goes into one pot with shared decisions), a joint initiative (one effort, coordinated funding) or co-funded grants. These are judgment calls from the sources. Years are when a program was announced or reported. The two handles set the first and last year shown; Play moves the end handle forward. Research as of Nov 18, 2026.

How Claude built this network visualization?

This version was rebuilt from the 55 prompts behind the original DDF Funders Network ↗. Every feature those prompts asked for is kept; the research was re-run on Nov 18, 2026.

    Checked and left out

    • Five-foundation payout pledge (June 2020). DDF, Ford, Kellogg, MacArthur and Mellon pledged over $1.7B in extra grantmaking; DDF's part was up to $100M, raised by issuing bonds. Each foundation gave on its own, so it is not a pooled program. Source ↗
    • Building Bridges. Its fellowship and documentary fund are DDF-funded and run with partners (Sundance, Islamic Scholarship Fund, CAAM). No other funder was found putting money in.
    • Not fully searched: DDF's full grants database. Some co-funded programs may be missing.
